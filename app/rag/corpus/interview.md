# Interview answers that land

## The STAR framework
Structure behavioural answers as Situation, Task, Action, Result. Situation sets
the scene in one or two sentences. Task states what you specifically were
responsible for. Action is the bulk of the answer and must be in first person
singular — what *you* did, not what the team did. Result closes with a measurable
outcome. Most weak answers fail at Task (unclear what they owned) and Result (no
number).

## Say "I", not "we"
Interviewers are hiring you, not your former team. "We redesigned the onboarding
flow" tells them nothing about your contribution. "I owned the three screens after
signup, and I ran the A/B test that picked the final design" does. Use "we" only
for genuine context, then switch back.

## Quantify the result
An answer without a number is an anecdote. Signups up 18%, support tickets down
from 400 to 260 a week, release time cut from two days to twenty minutes. If you
genuinely have no metric, use a decision outcome instead: "that analysis is why we
killed the feature".

## Length and pacing
Aim for 150-250 words, roughly 60-90 seconds spoken. Under 60 seconds reads as
thin preparation; over two minutes and the interviewer stops listening and starts
waiting. Practise out loud with a timer — answers always run longer aloud than
they look on the page.

## Failure questions
When asked about a failure, pick a real one with genuine consequences, own your
part without deflecting to circumstances or other people, and spend most of the
answer on what changed in your behaviour afterwards. Candidates who choose a fake
weakness ("I care too much") lose credibility for the rest of the interview.

## Questions to ask them
Always have three, and make them specific to what you heard in the interview.
"What does the first ninety days look like for this role?" and "What is the thing
about this team that surprises new joiners?" show engagement. "What does the
company do?" shows you did not prepare.
