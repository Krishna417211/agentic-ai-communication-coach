# Clarity, tone and everyday professional writing

## Cut hedging
"I just wanted to quickly check if maybe you might have had a chance to possibly
look at this?" contains five hedges and zero information. Hedging is meant to
sound polite, but it makes a request easy to ignore and makes the writer sound
uncertain of their own standing. "Have you had a chance to review the draft?" is
both clearer and no less polite.

## Prefer active voice
"The report was reviewed and changes were made" hides who did what. "I reviewed
the report and made three changes" is shorter and assigns responsibility. Passive
voice is occasionally right — when the actor is genuinely unknown or irrelevant —
but in professional writing it is usually a way of avoiding ownership.

## Delete the filler
Just, actually, basically, really, very, simply, honestly, obviously. Each one
either adds nothing or subtly insults the reader ("obviously" implies they should
already have known). Deleting them almost never changes the meaning, which is the
proof they were not needed.

## Sentence length
Average 15 to 20 words. Vary the rhythm — a short sentence after a long one lands
hard. Any sentence over 40 words should be split; if you cannot split it, you have
not decided what you are saying.

## Match register to relationship
Formality is a dial, not a virtue. Over-formal writing to a close colleague reads
as cold or passive-aggressive; casual writing to a regulator reads as careless.
Match the register of the person you are writing to, then move half a step more
formal if the stakes are high.

## Avoid jargon that carries no information
"Leverage synergies to move the needle going forward" says nothing. Business
jargon is most often used to sound authoritative while avoiding a specific claim.
Replace each phrase with what you actually mean, and if you cannot, you did not
mean anything.

## Read it back as the recipient
Before sending anything consequential, read it once as the person receiving it,
in the worst mood they might plausibly be in. Most tone disasters are visible
within ten seconds of doing this.
