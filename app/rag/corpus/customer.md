# Customer and client communication

## Acknowledge before you explain
An upset customer needs to know they were heard before they can absorb an
explanation. Lead with acknowledgement of the impact on them, then what you are
doing, then when they will next hear from you. Leading with the explanation reads
as an excuse, however accurate it is.

## Never blame the customer
"You must have entered the wrong details" ends the relationship even when it is
true. "Let me check what happened with that entry" gets to the same place without
the accusation. If the customer did make the mistake, fix it first and educate
second.

## Avoid policy-shield language
"Unfortunately that is our policy" tells the customer you will not help and have
no reason to give. Explain the actual constraint, and say what you *can* do:
"I can't refund an order after 30 days, but I can send a replacement part at no
charge — would that work?" Always pair a no with an alternative.

## Apologise once, specifically, and move on
One specific apology beats five vague ones. "I'm sorry the order arrived four days
late" is credible. Repeated "so sorry for the inconvenience" reads as a script and
makes the customer angrier. Never apologise in a way that admits liability you
have not verified.

## Commit only to what you control
Give a date you can hit, not the date the customer wants to hear. A missed
commitment costs more trust than a disappointing but accurate one. If you cannot
give a resolution date, give a next-update date instead: "I'll write to you on
Thursday even if I don't have the fix yet."

## Close the loop
Follow up after the resolution to confirm it actually worked. This is the cheapest
trust-building action available and almost nobody does it.
