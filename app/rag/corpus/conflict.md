# Handling conflict and difficult conversations

## Separate the person from the problem
Attack the problem, never the person. "The report arrived after the deadline and
that pushed the client meeting" describes an event. "You are always late" is a
character judgement, and character judgements make people defend themselves
instead of solving the problem.

## Use "I" statements, not "you" statements
"You never update the tracker" invites denial. "I couldn't tell where the project
stood on Monday, and I had to guess in the client call" states an impact the other
person cannot argue with, because it happened to you. The formula is:
when X happened, the effect on me was Y, and what I need is Z.

## Ban the escalators
Certain phrases predictably escalate: "always", "never", "obviously", "as I
already told you", "with all due respect", "calm down", and any sentence starting
with "you people". Delete them before sending. If a sentence would embarrass you
if it were forwarded to the whole team, rewrite it.

## Acknowledge before you disagree
People cannot hear a counterargument until they believe they have been heard.
Restate the other person's position in your own words first, accurately enough
that they would agree with your summary, and only then say what you see
differently. "You need this shipped by Friday because the client demo is booked —
I understand. Here is what worries me about that date."

## Choose the right channel
Never resolve a real conflict over email. Written conflict escalates because tone
is invisible and both sides re-read the worst possible interpretation. Use email
only to schedule the conversation and to summarise what was agreed afterwards.

## Close with a concrete next step
End every difficult conversation with an agreed action, an owner and a date.
Without one, both people leave with different memories of what was decided, and
the same conflict returns in two weeks with more resentment attached.
